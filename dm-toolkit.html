DM Overview
Everything you need before the session begins
🤫 THE BIG SECRET — Don't read aloud! The Festival Star wasn't stolen. It rolled away when a baby dragon named Pip sneezed on it by accident. Pip is hiding in the Whispering Woods, curled around the Star, trying to warm it back up. The Star isn't glowing because it's scared. The kids must be kind to Pip — not fight — and the Star will glow again.
Adventure Premise
The Lost Star Festival
Every 100 years, the Glimmering Kingdom holds the Star Festival. The Festival Star — a giant warm glowing gem — has vanished. Professor Hoot, the ancient owl Star Keeper, begs the heroes to find it before sunset. Along the way they'll cross a glowing marsh, solve a riddle in a cave, and follow tracks through a whispering forest.
Session Vitals
🕐 Duration: ~90 minutes
👧👦 Players: 2 children (ages 5 & 7)
👨 GU Role: All NPCs, creatures & narration
🎲 Dice: d6 only (count 4,5,6 as ⭐)
💡 Core Lesson: Kindness is more powerful than strength
🌍 Setting: The Glimmering Kingdom

🗺️
The Adventure
The Hunt for the Lost Star — 4 locations, any order
🧭 Opening Read-Aloud "The Glimmering Kingdom is buzzing with excitement — the Star Festival starts at sunset! But disaster has struck. Professor Hoot, the very old and very wise owl who keeps the Festival Star safe, has discovered it is gone. Without it, no wishes can be made. The Professor has asked YOU, brave heroes, to find it before the sun goes down. Are you ready?"
⚜ LOCATION 1 ⚜
STOP 1
🦉 Professor Hoot's Observatory

A tall crooked tower crammed with star charts, half-eaten biscuits, and towers of paper. The Professor is very old and very forgetful — his glasses are always on his head.

🎭 How to Play Professor Hoot Speak in a slow, grand, wavery voice. Keep interrupting yourself to look for things ("now where did I put… ah! Never mind."). Kids love helping him find things — let them "discover" his missing map or glasses.
🧩 Challenge — Paper Sort (Smart Roll) Shuffle 5–7 index cards face-down. One is marked "MAP ★" on the back. Kids flip them one at a time to find it. For the 5-year-old: mark the map card with a star so it's clearly different. For the 7-year-old: describe each card ("this one has a picture of a cat…") and have them remember what they've seen before finding the map card. This counts as a Smart Challenge — no dice needed if they succeed at the real activity.
Smart Challenge Reward: Starlight Lantern (3★)
⚜ LOCATION 2 ⚜
STOP 2
🐸 The Bubble Marsh

A bouncy, glowing swamp where giant lily pads float on bubbles. It's beautiful but easy to sink into. Cheerful frogs are everywhere, easily distracted by shiny things.

🧩 Challenge — Even-Number Lily Pads (Smart/Fast) Draw or write numbers 1–10 on paper "lily pads." Lay them on the floor. Kids must hop only on the even ones (2,4,6,8,10) to cross. For the 5-year-old: use just 1–6. For the 7-year-old: use 1–12 and add a rule: "skip number 8, the frog is sitting on it!"
💡 GU Tip — The Bubble Frogs Frogs are friendly but keep bumping into the heroes. They'll clear a path if given something shiny (a coin, a button, even a crinkled piece of foil from your pocket). Kids love improvising this kind of bribe!
Smart + Fast Challenge Reward: Marsh Boots (2★)
⚜ LOCATION 3 ⚜
STOP 3
🦇 The Candy Crag Caves

A cave made entirely of crystal that catches the light and looks like candy. Rhyme Bats live here — friendly bats who ONLY speak in rhymes and squeak with delight when someone rhymes back.

🧩 Challenge — The Rhyme Bat Riddle You (the GU) speak in rhymes the whole scene — even badly, the kids will laugh. The lead Rhyme Bat says:

"We will let you through, it's true, it's true — but first you must do what rhymers do! I'll start a rhyme, you finish mine: The dragon flew over the tall blue hill, and landed right next to a big old…"

Any rhyme with "hill" works. Accept everything. Then do 2 more. The 5-year-old can have simpler ones ("the cat sat on the…").
Smart Challenge Reward: Bat Echo Stone (2★)
⚜ LOCATION 4 ⚜
STOP 4
🌳 The Whispering Woods

A forest where the trees have gentle, whispery faces and share secrets with those who are quiet enough to listen. Pip the Baby Dragon is hiding here, very embarrassed.

🧩 Challenge — The Silence Test "The whispering trees will only tell you where Pip is hiding if you can be completely silent for 10 whole seconds." Start a timer. Count silently on your fingers. This is a genuinely fun real-world test and 5-year-olds find it very hard — let them try 2–3 times. Celebrate loudly when they succeed!
🤫 Finding Pip Pip is curled around the Star trying to warm it up. He looks up with huge, teary eyes. He immediately says "I'm sorry! I'm sorry! I didn't mean to sneeze it away!" He's clearly not a villain — just a frightened baby dragon. The Star will glow again only if the kids choose to comfort Pip, not threaten or capture him.
⚠️ If Kids Try to Fight Pip Pip doesn't fight back — he just cries and curls up smaller. Gently narrate: "Pip looks so sad and scared. He whispers: 'Please don't hurt me…' The Star flickers even dimmer." This nudges them toward kindness without forcing it.
No dice — pure roleplay Reward: Pip joins the party! (3★)
⚜ FINALE ⚜
The Finale — The Star Festival
🌟 Return to the Kingdom

The heroes return with Pip and the glowing Star. Professor Hoot cries happy tears ("I knew you'd do it! Now where did I put my invitation to the party…"). The Festival begins!

✅ GOOD ENDING — Pip was welcomed The King is delighted. Pip gets to light the fireworks with a (controlled) sneeze. Everyone receives a "Friend of the Kingdom" medal. Pip stays as a companion.
🔶 PARTIAL ENDING — Pip was captured The King looks sad. "A frightened creature is no gift." He orders Pip released. Heroes still get a reward but the GU can gently discuss why Pip might have felt sad.
💡 The Lesson to Land Whether the ending is perfect or not, find a moment to say in character: "The Kingdom's greatest hero tonight wasn't the strongest or the fastest — it was the one who chose to be kind."

⚜ TEACHING MOMENT RULE ⚜
⭐ The Teaching Moment — Growing a Shine
If a player rolls a Star on every single die in their pool, that's a Teaching Moment! The character has mastered something new. The player can add +1 to an existing Shine, or write a brand new Shine at 1 point. This is a powerful, exciting moment — celebrate it! Say: "Something clicks — you've never felt so natural doing that. Write down [Shine name] — you just got better at it forever."

Shy, apologetic, and adorably clumsy. Pip sneezes sparks when nervous and has huge teary eyes. He genuinely didn't mean to sneeze the Star away and feels absolutely terrible about it.

Challenges
Real-world activities calibrated for ages 5 and 7
📌 Design Philosophy Each challenge pairs an in-game dice roll with a real physical or mental activity the kids actually do. When they succeed at the real activity, they succeed in the game — no dice needed. Dice can be used as backup or for dramatic tension. The 5-year-old always gets an easier version.
🔒
The Colour Lock
PuzzleAges 4+Smart Roll
A magical door has 3 coloured crystals that must glow in the right order.
🎮 How to Run It Show kids 3 coloured objects (blocks, cups). Show the sequence, then cover them. They recall the order. Age 5: 3 items. Age 7: 4 items, with a distraction in between.
🌉
The Wobbly Bridge
PhysicalAges 3+Fast Roll
A rope bridge over a rushing river shakes with every step!
🎮 How to Run It 5 pillows in a line on the floor. Kids cross without stepping off. Add rule: every 3rd pillow, freeze for 3 seconds. If they fall off, they "splash" and start again — keep it fun, not punishing.
🧌
The Sleeping Giant's Riddle
RiddleAges 5+Smart Roll
A very sleepy giant won't move unless you answer his question.
🎮 Riddles by Age Age 5: "I have four legs and you sleep in me at night. What am I?" (A bed!)
Age 7: "The more you take, the more you leave behind. What am I?" (Footsteps!)
🐰
The Animal Whisperer
RoleplayAges 3+No Dice
A frightened baby animal blocks the path and won't move.
🎮 How to Run It GU acts as a scared bunny, bird, or fox. Kids must speak softly, crouch down, offer pretend food. If they're genuinely gentle in how they act and speak, they succeed — no dice. Reward kindness, not strategy.
⭐
The Star Map Maze
DrawingAges 4+Smart Roll
A magical map appears but has paths missing — trace the route!
🎮 How to Run It Draw a simple maze on paper beforehand. Age 5: 3 turns, wide paths. Age 7: 5 turns, narrower paths, one dead end. They trace with a finger or pencil. Let them retry — the journey matters, not the time.
👏
The Echo Cave Clap
MemoryAges 4+Smart Roll
The cave echoes back a clapping pattern — repeat it to open the secret door!
🎮 How to Run It Tap a rhythm on the table: clap-clap-pause-clap. Kids repeat it exactly. Age 5: 3 beats. Age 7: 5 beats. Use a spoon on a bowl for a dramatic cave-echo sound! Get it right in 3 tries or the cave giggles and resets.
⚜ FAILURE IS OKAY ⚜
💡 When Kids Fail a Challenge
In FirstFable, failure never stops the story — it makes it more interesting. If kids roll no Stars, something goes wrong that makes the next step slightly harder, but they never get stuck. Examples: they miss the lily pad and get their boots wet (–1 die to the next Fast roll), they fail the riddle and the bats make them sing a silly song first, they fail the silence test and get three more tries. Always frame failure as a funny setback, never a punishment.

Mini-Games
Physical activities — run one every 20–25 minutes to reset energy
🕐 Timing Guide Kids ages 5–7 lose focus after about 20 minutes of table talk. Run a mini-game at each of these points: after the Observatory, after the Marsh, and after the Cave. Each takes 5–8 minutes and earns a small in-game reward.
🐲
Pip's Sneeze Training
Baby dragon Pip needs to learn to aim his sneezes — but he keeps accidentally blowing things away!
🎮 How to Play Set up 5 paper cups or pom-poms in a row on the floor. Kids blow through a straw from 1 metre away to knock them over. How many can each hero knock down? Whoever knocks the most "teaches Pip" and earns +1 to Pip's Fire Sneeze Stars for the session.
Materials: Straws + cups/pom-poms Reward: +1 Pip Star
💎
Pirate Treasure Sort
A pirate chest spilled everywhere! Sort the treasure to find the hidden magic gem before time runs out.
🎮 How to Play Mix 15 small objects of 3 colours. Kids race to sort them into 3 groups. Hidden in the pile: one special "gem" (a button, coin, or painted rock). First to find it while sorting wins a Star. For the 5-year-old: fewer objects (9), only 2 colours.
Materials: Small coloured objects + 1 special gem Reward: Earns 1 extra Star (any item)
🏰
Knight's Freeze Training
All knights must learn to stop instantly on command. This is freeze training — and it's harder than it looks.
🎮 How to Play Play music. When it stops (you control this), everyone freezes like a statue — including you! Last to freeze loses a point. First to reach 5 points becomes Knight Captain for the session and gets the in-game title "Hero of the Freeze." Play for 3–4 rounds.
Materials: Any music source Reward: Title "Knight Captain" + 1 Star (Roland)
💫
The Faerie Ring Dance
Sela teaches the heroes a special faerie dance that reveals invisible paths through the forest.
🎮 How to Play GU calls out movements one at a time, building a sequence: "Spin once! Jump twice! Touch your nose! Clap three times! Turn around! Stomp once!" Kids follow along. If they complete the full 6-step sequence, a "hidden path" is revealed (you point to a new area or skip a challenge). Younger child can do 3 steps.
Materials: Nothing needed Reward: Shortcut through next location
🦁
Animal Sounds Relay
Ruby's animals are lost in the forest! Call them home by making their sounds. Get 5 right to rescue them all.
🎮 How to Play GU whispers an animal to one child. They make the sound without speaking — other child guesses the animal. Swap. Each correct guess = one animal returns home. Reach 5 to rescue all of Ruby's animals and earn a bonus Star on Blaze. Include fun ones: dolphin, elephant, tiny mouse, very angry cat.
Materials: Nothing needed Reward: +1 Blaze Star (Ruby)

Quick Rules Reference
FirstFable system — everything at a glance
Core Mechanic
How to Roll
  1. GU decides which Stat applies
  2. Player decides if any Shines or Weaknesses apply
  3. Roll = Stat + Shines − Weakness dice
  4. Every die showing 4, 5, or 6 = one ⭐ Star
  5. Count the Stars — more = better result
Star Outcomes
What Stars Mean
StarsResult
0 ⭐Failure — something goes wrong, situation gets harder
1 ⭐Barely success — achieved, but barely, with a complication
2 ⭐Clean success — it works, no complications
3 ⭐Full success — works perfectly
5+ ⭐Amazing — everybody watching is impressed
⭐ on ALL diceTeaching Moment — gain a new Shine!
⚜ TYPES OF ACTION ⚜
Tasks
Simple actions the character can do pretty easily. No dice rolled. No Shine gain possible. Tasks can set up Challenges — if a Task makes a Challenge easier, the player gets +1 die on their Challenge roll.

Example: Sela helps guide the party through briars by taking a Task to find the lightest thorns — everyone else gets +1 die to cross.
Challenges
Something important with a reasonable chance of going wrong. Dice are rolled. Stars are earned. Teaching Moments can happen.

Challenges can be: Normal (one character vs a difficulty), Contest (two characters competing — most Stars wins), or Fight (see below).
Fights
A special Contest. Both sides set their Damage number first (max = all Stats + all Shines = 12). Attacker rolls, defender rolls — attacker's Stars minus defender's Stars = Damage dealt. Reach your Damage limit = Out (unconscious, scared off, gives up).

No one dies in FirstFable. "Out" means they're done for the scene.
Teaching Moment
Roll a Star on every die you rolled (not just some). When this happens:

1. The action succeeds brilliantly
2. The player adds +1 to an existing Shine, OR writes a new Shine at 1 point

This is huge — celebrate it loudly! It's the game's growth mechanic and kids remember Teaching Moments for a long time.
⚜ HURT & DAMAGE ⚜
The Hurt Rule
A character becomes Hurt if they take more Damage Stars than the value of their highest Stat or Shine. Hurt characters take −1 die to all Challenges and gain a temporary Weakness.

To avoid being Hurt: set your Damage lower (e.g. 3). You'll drop Out sooner, but you won't be Hurt. A new character with all stats at max can take up to 12 Damage before going Out — but risks being badly Hurt.

For kids' games: Keep Damage simple. Most fights won't even happen — use kindness, cleverness, and running away instead.
